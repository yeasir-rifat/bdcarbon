Biochar Carbon Removal

Turn agricultural residues into long-term carbon storage and sustainable revenue.

What we offer

Bangladesh generates large volumes of agricultural residues, including rice husk, jute stick, bagasse and crop straw, much of which is burned in the open or left to decompose. Converting this biomass into biochar through pyrolysis locks carbon into a stable form for the long term, improves soil health and creates new income opportunities for farmers and industry.

  • 01Feedstock availability and supply-chain mapping at district and upazila level
  • 02Pyrolysis technology selection and unit-sizing advisory
  • 03Project design documentation aligned with leading biochar methodologies
  • 04Biochar application trials and agronomic co-benefit assessment
  • 05Baseline, permanence and leakage assessment
  • 06Registry pathway guidance and validation and verification readiness
  • 07Community benefit-sharing and stakeholder engagement design

Biochar CDR Services

Distributed / Smallholder-Led Biochar

Decentralized, farm-gate pyrolysis with low-cost kilns brings climate finance directly to rural communities.

Bangladesh produces millions of tons of unmanaged agricultural residues that can be converted into biochar to restore soil health. We provide smallholder aggregation frameworks, field protocols and community-level MRV systems that make projects verification-ready.

Industrial-Scale Biochar

High-throughput, centralized pyrolysis facilities with integrated thermal energy recovery can convert agro-industrial residues, such as those from rice, jute and sugar mills, into durable carbon removal at scale. We provide end-to-end support covering feedstock sourcing, plant engineering, techno-economic feasibility and registry-ready crediting.

Home>ERW

Enhanced Rock Weathering (ERW)

Harnessing natural minerals for durable carbon removal.

What we offer

Enhanced Rock Weathering accelerates a natural geological process. Finely crushed silicate rock is applied to agricultural land, where it removes CO₂ from the atmosphere while supporting soil health and crop productivity. With extensive agricultural landscapes and potential mineral resources, Bangladesh offers promising opportunities for science-based carbon removal projects.

ERW Services

Agricultural ERW Projects

We support the deployment of enhanced rock weathering in agricultural systems through site screening, agronomic planning and stakeholder engagement, helping identify opportunities where carbon removal and agricultural benefits can be delivered together.

Carbon Removal Potential

ERW delivers durable carbon removal by accelerating natural weathering processes. It is increasingly recognized as a leading carbon dioxide removal (CDR) approach with potential for long-term climate impact.

Why Bangladesh?

Large agricultural areas, diverse soil conditions and growing climate ambition create opportunities to explore ERW as part of sustainable land management and carbon removal strategies.

Article 6 and NDC 3.0: what they mean for Bangladesh

A short guide to the climate policy language shaping carbon market chances in Bangladesh.

Nationally Determined Contributions (NDCs) are each country’s own climate targets under the Paris Agreement, renewed over time. NDC 3.0 refers to the next round of these commitments.

Article 6 sets out how countries can cooperate to meet those targets, including through carbon markets. For project developers, understanding both helps show which projects fit national priorities and how credits may be recognised.

Carbon literacy in five minutes: emissions and offsets

A plain-language start for anyone new to carbon: what we emit, what absorbs it and what offsets do.

Carbon literacy starts with three ideas. Emissions are the greenhouse gases we release when we burn fuel, farm and clear land. Sinks, such as forests, soils and oceans, absorb carbon from the air.

Offsets and credits fund projects that avoid or remove emissions elsewhere. They work best as a complement to cutting our own emissions first, never as a substitute for it.

From design to issuance: the path of a carbon credit

How a project idea becomes a verified, tradable carbon credit, explained clearly step by step.

A carbon credit begins as a project idea. Developers design the project, measure a baseline and follow a recognised methodology. An independent body then validates the design.

Once the project is running, its results are monitored and verified. A registry issues credits for verified reductions or removals, which buyers can then purchase and retire.

Three tests every credible carbon project has to pass

Additionality, permanence and leakage: the checks that separate strong projects from the rest.

Additionality asks whether the emission reduction would have happened anyway. If it would, the project adds no climate benefit. Permanence asks how long the carbon stays out of the atmosphere.

Leakage checks that emissions are not simply shifted somewhere else. Strong projects also protect communities and share benefits fairly, which is why safeguards sit alongside the carbon accounting.

Feedstock, pyrolysis and permanence: biochar in soil

Why farm residues such as rice husk are a good starting point for durable carbon removal.

Biochar is made by heating biomass such as rice husk or jute sticks with very little oxygen, a process called pyrolysis. Instead of burning or rotting and releasing carbon dioxide, much of the carbon is held in a stable solid form.

When added to soil, biochar can stay there for a long time and may improve soil health. A credible project maps its feedstock, documents how the biochar is used and measures permanence carefully.
