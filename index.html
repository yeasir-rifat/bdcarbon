Carbon: Our Common Ground

Carbon sits at the centre of the climate story: from global warming and greenhouse gas emissions to fossil-fuel dependence and a just transition. It is also a growing opportunity through carbon markets, crediting and green business. The Bangladesh Carbon Network Platform is a nationally rooted, globally connected hub that brings together education, research, innovation, project development and consultancy, so youth, researchers and educators can lead in carbon literacy, climate resilience and market participation.

We support Bangladesh's net-zero ambitions and align our work with:

  • NDC 3.0
  • Article 6 mechanisms
  • National Adaptation Plan (NAP)
  • Biennial Update / Transparency reporting
  • Bangladesh Carbon Framework

Agricultural Landscaping

Greener fields, stronger farmers, climate-smart landscapes.

What we offer

We support climate-smart agriculture, Good Agricultural Practices and organic farming, from planning to farmer training.

  • Climate-smart agriculture planning and farmer adoption
  • GAP training and certification readiness support
  • Organic farming transition support and input guidance
  • Landscape and resource mapping: feedstock, water, soil

AWD Rice Projects

Cut rice methane emissions while saving field water.

What we offer

We design AWD carbon projects that work for smallholders, from field protocols to farmer aggregation and monitoring.

  • Project area screening and eligibility assessment
  • Farmer aggregation and grouped-project design
  • AWD field protocols and training modules
  • Emission baseline and reduction estimation
  • dMRV set-up for water-level and practice verification
  • Fair benefit-sharing model design for farmers
  • Methodology selection and registry support

Our potential

Rice is the backbone of Bangladesh agriculture, and irrigated Boro rice covers millions of hectares. Alternate Wetting and Drying (AWD) can cut methane emissions and irrigation costs, making it a strong nature-based carbon opportunity.

Biochar CDR

Turn Bangladesh's biomass into durable carbon removal.

What we offer

We help you assess feedstock, select pyrolysis technology, design the project and prepare it for credit registration.

  • Feedstock and supply-chain mapping at upazila level
  • Pyrolysis technology and unit-sizing advisory
  • Project design documents aligned with biochar methods
  • Biochar trials and agronomic co-benefit assessment
  • Baseline, permanence and leakage assessment
  • Registry pathway, validation and verification readiness
  • Community benefit-sharing and stakeholder engagement

Our potential

Bangladesh generates agricultural residues every year, such as rice husk, jute sticks and crop straw, much of it burned or left to decompose. This is a rich feedstock base for biochar CDR projects that also improve soil health and farmer income.

dMRV Support

Digital monitoring and verification you can trust.

What we offer

We build and advise on dMRV systems that make your carbon project data trusted, transparent and verification-ready.

  • dMRV system architecture and tool selection
  • Mobile data collection and farmer registry set-up
  • Remote sensing and satellite data integration
  • Data quality assurance and audit trail design
  • Dashboard and reporting system development
  • Training for field teams and project staff
  • Alignment with registry and Article 6 reporting needs

Our potential

High-integrity carbon credits depend on high-quality data. Digital MRV (dMRV) uses satellites, sensors, mobile apps and analytics to make monitoring faster, cheaper and more transparent, which is vital for dispersed smallholder projects.

Lower livestock methane. Raise livestock productivity.

What we offer

We help build livestock methane-reduction projects: from herd data and feed strategy to credible monitoring.

  • Livestock population and herd-system baseline study
  • Feed ration and additive strategy advisory

ERW Projects

Let crushed rocks pull carbon out of the sky safely.

What we offer

We support site suitability, feedstock sourcing, field trials and measurement planning for rock weathering projects.

  • Land and soil suitability screening and mapping
  • Rock feedstock sourcing and logistics assessment
  • Field trial design and soil and water sampling plan
  • Carbon removal quantification approach design
  • Farmer engagement and safety / environmental screening
  • Co-benefit evaluation (yield, soil pH, nutrient supply)
  • Protocol selection and registry pathway guidance

Our potential

Enhanced Rock Weathering (ERW) spreads crushed silicate rock on farmland to lock away atmospheric CO2. Bangladesh's warm, humid climate and widespread agricultural land make it a promising place for ERW research and early deployment.

Suitability Reporting

Know exactly where and what to build before investing.

What we offer

We deliver suitability reports and ESG baseline and endline surveys that guide investors, developers and policymakers.

  • Project suitability matrix: technical, social, economic
  • Agro-ecological zone and land-use analysis
  • ESG baseline survey of starting conditions
  • ESG endline survey: change and impact at project close
  • Sustainability matrix and indicator framework design
  • ISO-aligned (e.g. ISO 14064) GHG accounting guidance
  • Pre-feasibility and market-readiness reports
  • Stakeholder and policy briefs (NDC 3.0, Article 6)

Our potential

Not every landscape suits every carbon project. A suitability assessment shows which agro-ecological zones, practices and communities offer the best climate, social and business returns across Bangladesh.

Article 6 and NDC 3.0: what they mean for Bangladesh

A short guide to the climate policy language shaping carbon market chances in Bangladesh.

Nationally Determined Contributions (NDCs) are each country’s own climate targets under the Paris Agreement, renewed over time. NDC 3.0 refers to the next round of these commitments.

Article 6 sets out how countries can cooperate to meet those targets, including through carbon markets. For project developers, understanding both helps show which projects fit national priorities and how credits may be recognised.

Carbon literacy in five minutes: emissions and offsets

A plain-language start for anyone new to carbon: what we emit, what absorbs it and what offsets do.

Carbon literacy starts with three ideas. Emissions are the greenhouse gases we release when we burn fuel, farm and clear land. Sinks, such as forests, soils and oceans, absorb carbon from the air.

Offsets and credits fund projects that avoid or remove emissions elsewhere. They work best as a complement to cutting our own emissions first, never as a substitute for it.

From design to issuance: the path of a carbon credit

How a project idea becomes a verified, tradable carbon credit, explained clearly step by step.

A carbon credit begins as a project idea. Developers design the project, measure a baseline and follow a recognised methodology. An independent body then validates the design.

Once the project is running, its results are monitored and verified. A registry issues credits for verified reductions or removals, which buyers can then purchase and retire.

Three tests every credible carbon project has to pass

Additionality, permanence and leakage: the checks that separate strong projects from the rest.

Additionality asks whether the emission reduction would have happened anyway. If it would, the project adds no climate benefit. Permanence asks how long the carbon stays out of the atmosphere.

Leakage checks that emissions are not simply shifted somewhere else. Strong projects also protect communities and share benefits fairly, which is why safeguards sit alongside the carbon accounting.

Feedstock, pyrolysis and permanence: biochar in soil

Why farm residues such as rice husk are a good starting point for durable carbon removal.

Biochar is made by heating biomass such as rice husk or jute sticks with very little oxygen, a process called pyrolysis. Instead of burning or rotting and releasing carbon dioxide, much of the carbon is held in a stable solid form.

When added to soil, biochar can stay there for a long time and may improve soil health. A credible project maps its feedstock, documents how the biochar is used and measures permanence carefully.
